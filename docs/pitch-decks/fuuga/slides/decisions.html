Typed decisions answer the two objections every buyer raises: we have no training data, and we cannot tell whether a new model is better. Recorded outcomes are the training data, and a decision has a right answer to score against. Detail — Outcomes: approved, declined, fraud, which team took the case. — Scored with accuracy, log loss, Brier score and calibration error on held-out cases. — Above the confidence threshold the software acts; below it, a person does. The share automated is the return. — For prose, the judge is typed too: old against new as one closed choice, asked both ways round. Answers — How do you know the retrain helped? The typed judge compares old and new on held-out cases; public benchmarks are checked as well. — What accuracy do customers get? Measured per customer, on their own held-out cases, inside their environment.
