Hietanen Consultancy · Fuuga · 15

A solo founder with AI leverage is either a risk or a thesis, and the honest version is the one that survives diligence. Detail — Tuomas Hietanen: CTO and co-founder of Finular Group for nine years, building and running two independently regulated lenders at the same time — Fund Ourselves in the UK and WeLendUs in the US. Lead engineer at Basware on purchase-to-pay at global scale. Maintainer of SQLProvider and LINQKit, published .NET author, and the author of Fuuga. — Method: one engineer directing AI coding agents, every module under unit and property tests, the whole source public, funded by consultancy income rather than a round. It is the method the product sells, run on ourselves. — Ownership today: the founder owns sales, ML delivery, the platform and compliance. A network of senior engineers we have shipped with joins an engagement on contract when the work needs more hands, so delivery is not capped at one person; names on request, not on the slide. — Ownership next: platform engineering is the first role to move off the founder, then ML delivery and sales, then compliance. Each function passes to a hire when signed revenue pays for it — engineers, ML engineers and a data lead, a seller for regulated enterprise and a compliance lead. A round brings them forward: four engineers at close, a head of sales and two ML engineers from month seven, then a developer-relations lead for partner enablement. — Key-person risk: the code is public domain and under test, every model's recipe is written down and rerunnable, and the first hires — or engineers from the network — can run that loop without the founder. Common questions — "Can it be verified?" The codebase, the test suites and the commit history are open. A technical partner can check the whole claim in an afternoon. — "Who else is involved?" A second senior team member is identified, with partners for data, for compliance and for ML advisory. Each name goes on this slide once that agreement is in writing, and not before.
