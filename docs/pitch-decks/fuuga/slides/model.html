Business model

The tool is not the skill. We keep the skill in the building as your team changes.

01 · Engagements

Decision engagements

Fixed scope on outcomes the customer already records, ending with a calibrated engine in production and a team of theirs who can repeat it. Profitable today, and the source of every model, recipe and measured number.

02 · Continuity

Supported builds and continuity

Signed builds, long-term support branches, advisories, an SLA and the provenance record — plus the part that matters over years: when their people change, we bring the next ones up to speed on their own models. [£__k] a year.

03 · Partners

Enablement and certification

Other consultancies implement Fuuga; we train and certify them, and they escalate to us. Delivery grows without our headcount, and every partner sells our support alongside their own work.

04 · Licence

Embedded OEM licence

A vendor ships Fuuga inside their product and redistributes it across every install. Real money when it lands, priced last because nothing public prices it. Upside, not the plan.

Public domain is what makes the ladder work. Free adoption is how implementers arrive, and implementers are how delivery scales past the people we employ. A proprietary licence would protect the smallest line on this slide by killing the ecosystem that grows the other three.

Hietanen Consultancy · Fuuga · 09 · Engagements ~45% gross margin; continuity, partner and licence revenue ~80%

The order is the argument: engagements fund the company and produce the evidence, continuity is the line that compounds, partners break the headcount ceiling, and the OEM licence is upside. Detail — The analogy that lands: a stick is free, and that is not a furniture business. Timber costs nothing compared with a joiner who knows which cut holds. A capable toolchain in unpractised hands is a hazard rather than a shortcut — a miscalibrated model looks fine right up until it is audited. — The judgement is specific and tacit: which data mixture, what the eval is actually testing, where the calibration threshold sits, which quantisation survived, why a run was a regression. None of that is in the repository. — Continuity renews because the need recurs on its own: teams change. A team trained in year one is roughly half-replaced by year three at normal tenure, with nobody unhappy about anything. — Everything is documented, rerunnable and theirs. What is bought is that somebody stays current with the stack and can bring new engineers up to it — continuity, not dependence. Common questions — "Doesn't open source mean anyone can consult on this?" Yes, and at scale that is the point: implementers are distribution. We are upstream, we set the release branches, we are the escalation when a partner cannot diagnose something, and partners would rather resell our support than carry liability for a model's behaviour in a regulated client. — "Who pays for the GPUs?" Training runs on the customer's own hardware or cloud by default, so compute never sits on our margin. Where we are asked to arrange it, partner EU capacity is resold at a small margin and never counted as a growth line. Scope — The partner channel needs adoption we do not yet have. Near term other consultancies are competitors for the same deals, not partners, and the sequence cannot be skipped.
