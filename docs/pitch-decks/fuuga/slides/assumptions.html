Assumptions and risks

The drivers behind the numbers, and what could break them.

Drivers

  • We run it £100k a year — half of customers
  • They run it £60k a year — the other half
  • Partners £30k each: 5, then 20
  • Three sellers by year three, five closes each
  • Engineers £110k fully loaded

Risks, and our answer

  • In-region hosting suffices for most — we sell where it does not
  • Microsoft adds training — we plug into their interfaces
  • Key person — public code and tests make it transferable

Hietanen Consultancy · Fuuga · 19

Residency leads the risk column because it is the strongest objection, and the market slide already sizes for it. Drivers — Managed operation at £100k a year: one engineer at £110k fully loaded runs about three customers' engines, so the line earns about 60% gross margin. There is no public price for this service; it is our estimate and the first contracts set it. — Half of customers are assumed to have us run it. Regulated buyers without a model team lean that way; the split is the estimate most worth testing in the first ten sales. — Continuity at £60k a year for customers who run it themselves renews because turnover recurs: at two-to-three-year tenure, a team trained in year one is half-replaced by year three. — Partner enablement from year two: five implementation partners at £30k each for training, certification and escalation, twenty by year three. — OEM licences and per-token hosting are long-term and carry nothing in the numbers. — Running engines scales with headcount: seventeen customers in year three is about six engineers on operations, inside the eighteen at year end. — Quota-carrying sellers from month seven at five closes a year each: one in year one, three by year three. Three sellers bring fifteen of year three's twenty-three new organisations; certified partners bring the rest. — Five closes a seller is the honest middle: the median SaaS account executive quota is about $800k of new annual contract value, and only about half of them hit it. — Fully loaded engineer cost of £110k: a London median base of about £85k times a 1.25–1.4 on-cost. — Gross margin about 45% on consulting, about 60% on running it, about 80% on continuity and partner revenue. Training compute is bought by the customer, so it is not in the revenue line at all. Risks — A hosted tenancy in-region answers residency for most buyers. It does not answer software shipping into someone else's network, an air-gapped estate, or a regulator who wants evidence. — Microsoft closing the .NET gap: their tools call models; ours makes and measures them. Fuuga implements Microsoft.Extensions.AI and exports ONNX, so their progress widens the funnel. — Key-person dependency: the round funds senior hires with equity, and every module ships with unit and property tests, so the work is transferable rather than tribal. Common questions — "What is the sales cycle?" Two to three months for a vendor, six for a regulated buyer direct. — "Can you legally train on a hosted model's output?" Provider terms restrict it. Decision engines train on the customer's own recorded outcomes and need no teacher at all; counsel's view on the distillation path is in the data room. — "What about GPU cost volatility?" Consumer-GPU fine-tuning and CPU serving are core design goals, not workarounds.
