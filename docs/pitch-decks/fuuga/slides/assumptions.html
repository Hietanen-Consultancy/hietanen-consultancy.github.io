Assumptions and risks

The drivers behind the numbers, and what could break them.

Drivers

  • Supported builds and continuity [£60k] a year, renewing because turnover recurs: at two-to-three-year tenure a team trained in year one is half-replaced by year three
  • Partner enablement and certification from year two: [N] implementation partners at [£__k] each
  • Embedded OEM licence ACV [£80k] — upside, and unevidenced until the first vendor deal
  • Quota-carrying sellers from month [7] at [5] closes a year each — one in year one, [3] by year three to reach thirty
  • Fully loaded engineer cost [£110k] a year, London and remote mix
  • Gross margin ~80% on support, continuity and partner revenue, ~45% on engagements; training compute is bought by the customer, so it is not in the revenue line at all

Risks and how we answer them

  • A hosted tenancy in-region answers residency for most buyers. It does, and we do not pretend otherwise. It does not answer software shipping into someone else's network, an air-gapped estate, or a regulator who wants evidence. That is where we sell.
  • Microsoft closes the .NET inference gap. Their tools call models; ours makes and measures them. We implement Microsoft.Extensions.AI and export ONNX, so their progress widens the funnel.
  • Key-person dependency. The round funds senior hires with equity, and every module ships with unit and property tests so the work is transferable rather than tribal.

Residency leads the risk column because it is the strongest objection. A hosted tenancy in-region satisfies most compliance teams, and a deck that pretends otherwise is not worth reading. Common questions — "What is the sales cycle?" Two to three months for a vendor, six for a regulated buyer direct. Services are held flat as a share, with the consultancy team at four. — "Can you legally train on a hosted model's output?" Provider terms restrict it. Decision engines train on the customer's own recorded outcomes and need no teacher at all; counsel's view on the distillation path is in the data room. — "What if the code is forked commercially?" Buyers pay for support, signed builds and people, none of which fork. The moat slide develops this. — "What about GPU cost volatility?" Consumer-GPU fine-tuning and CPU serving are core design goals, not workarounds.
