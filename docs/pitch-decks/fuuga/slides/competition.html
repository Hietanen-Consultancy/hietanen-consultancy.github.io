Three answers this table is built to give. Detail — Python: the buyer does not have a Python team and will not get one approved. Unsloth, Axolotl and the HuggingFace stack are excellent, and they are a second toolchain owned by a specialist. — .NET libraries: Semantic Kernel, LLamaSharp, ONNX Runtime GenAI and ML.NET call models; they do not make them. — Microsoft's path: the route Microsoft offers .NET shops today is Copilot and Azure OpenAI, running OpenAI and Anthropic models in Microsoft's cloud, rented per request. It is the default most of our buyers already have, and the reason they cannot train on their own outcomes, keep the data in their network, or keep the model if the price changes. Fuuga implements Microsoft.Extensions.AI, so it sits alongside that path rather than against it. — Typed-output startups such as Jev validate the decision-engine category and raised real money for it. Each is a hosted service that keeps the weights, which is what the second and last rows measure. — Serving: Fuuga serves the OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible APIs together by default; the Python stack serves OpenAI's through a separate tool.
