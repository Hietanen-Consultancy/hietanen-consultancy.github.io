Serviceable market, built bottom-up from public registers rather than a top-down AI figure. The arithmetic — 4,834 EU credit institutions (ECB data, end 2024, as published in EBF Facts and Figures 2025) + 3,037 EEA insurance undertakings (EIOPA register, February 2024) + 708 deposit-takers and 584 insurers regulated by the UK PRA (Business Plan 2025/26) = 9,163 regulated banks and insurers. — About 30% run their core business systems on .NET: 29.9% of professional developers reported extensive C# work in the Stack Overflow 2025 survey, and .NET's share is higher than average in finance and insurance. That gives about 2,750 organisations. — About one in three of those cannot put their data into a hosted model at all: air-gapped estates, sovereignty requirements, or a supervisor who wants evidence of how the model was trained rather than a vendor statement. That gives about 916, shown as 900. — At £60k a year for supported builds and continuity, 916 organisations are worth about £55m a year, before any engagement revenue. That is the floor: at the projections' half-and-half mix with managed operation at £100k, the average is £80k and the market about £73m. The two shares are the estimate; the registers are fact. Each assumption can be tested by screening the list, and halving both still leaves a market thirty-five customers do not exhaust. Who else buys — Consultancies implementing Fuuga for their clients buy training, certification and an escalation path. — Nordic and smaller-language organisations, where hosted models underserve Finnish, Swedish and domain vocabularies. — Software vendors shipping on-premises .NET products into health, industry, the public sector and the defence supply chain — the later door, once the stack is proven in their customers' networks. Residency — Azure OpenAI inside a customer's own tenancy satisfies most compliance teams, and the one-in-three share reflects that. It does not satisfy an air-gapped estate, a vendor shipping software into a hospital network, or a regulator who wants evidence. That is the market.
