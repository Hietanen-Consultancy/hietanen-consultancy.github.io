Defensibility

Code is the cheap part now. We priced it accordingly.

The rate. A transformer, QLoRA, GGUF and continuous batching in F#, by one engineer directing AI agents, under test and in public — with Kimi K3 attention, GRPO and dynamic quantisation landing within weeks of the papers. A fork inherits the code, not the rate.

The next shape is a head, not a rewrite. One model representation, one training loop, one serving path — so a customer's next three AI problems run on the same stack, on the same hardware, under the same audit trail.

The record, and someone who can still read it. Anyone can support public-domain code. Nobody else holds a customer's calibration history, drift record and training provenance — or can bring their next three engineers up to speed on it once the team that built it has moved on.

Models and recipes. Trained models, calibration data and quantisation plans accumulate with every engagement and are not in the repository.

What a fork does not inherit

A customer's calibration history, a regulator's acceptance, twenty-five years of shipping into banks. None of those regenerate.

A buyer who could fork it still does not want to own a model team. What they pay for is accountability: supported builds, evidence that stays current, somebody named when the model is wrong.

And public domain answers their first question — can we audit it, and can we leave — before they ask it.

The slide is the business argument. This is the reasoning behind the licence. Detail — Source stopped being the advantage. An engineer with AI can regenerate a training loop — this one was. What stays scarce is domain judgement, the rate of execution, the record that accumulates in production, and the people who answer for it. Common questions — "Why give the code away?" A small company cannot defend source against a large one, and patents are published anyway. Releasing first puts the work in the public record as prior art, so nobody fences it off later. — "Won't a large vendor copy it?" F# is not a stack a large vendor lifts into its own product. The realistic risk was never a verbatim copy. — "Isn't owning the code an asset?" Code that has to be defended cannot be thrown away, and every proprietary line is maintenance that narrows what comes next. — "Why can't our own engineers do this with AI?" They can start. Finishing means carrying the maintenance, the evidence and the accountability, which is what the contract is for.
