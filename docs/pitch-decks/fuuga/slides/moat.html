The business argument behind the public-domain licence. Detail — The rate: a transformer, QLoRA, GGUF and continuous batching, by one engineer directing AI agents, under test and in public — with new attention variants, GRPO and dynamic quantisation landing within weeks of the papers. — The next shape is a head, not a rewrite: one model representation, one training loop, one serving path, so a customer's next three AI problems run on the same stack under the same audit trail. — The record: anyone can support public-domain code. Nobody else holds a customer's calibration history, drift record and training provenance, or can bring their next engineers up to speed on it once the team that built it has moved on. — Accountability: a buyer who could fork it still does not want to own a model team. What they pay for is supported builds, evidence that stays current, and somebody named when the model is wrong. Public domain answers their first question — can we audit it, and can we leave — before they ask it. — Source stopped being the advantage. What stays scarce is domain judgement, the rate of execution, the record that accumulates in production, and twenty-five years of shipping into banks. Common questions — "Why give the code away?" A small company cannot defend source against a large one. Releasing first puts the work in the public record as prior art, so nobody fences it off later. — "Won't a large vendor copy it?" The realistic risk was never a verbatim copy; a vendor's own stack moves at a vendor's pace. — "Why can't our own engineers do this with AI?" They can start. Finishing means carrying the maintenance, the evidence and the accountability, which is what the contract is for.
