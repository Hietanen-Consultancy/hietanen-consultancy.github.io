The problem

Enterprises rent their AI, and cannot maintain the AI they build.

Rented models, rising prices

Data leaves the company, every request is billed, and the model can be changed or retired. Today's prices are subsidised by investor capital; they will rise.

In-house models today are a specialist's side project

A data scientist builds them in Python tools the engineering team does not use. When that person leaves, nobody can maintain them.

Regulation asks who owns the model

The EU AI Act and DORA ask who trained the model, on what data, and who controls it.

The plain-language version of the thesis. Detail — Rented models: a hosted subscription sends customer records, contracts and code to a third party, bills every request, and can change or retire the model underneath a live product. Compliance teams in banks, insurers and public bodies decline the most valuable uses. — Prices: hosted inference is priced below cost to win market share. The largest provider reports losing more than it earns on the service, and analysts estimate API prices would need to rise several-fold to reach sustainable economics. A business building on today's per-token price is building on a subsidy. — Maintenance: the middle card is the problem every CIO has already met. In-house model work is done by a specialist in a separate toolchain, ships as a one-off, and the business systems it should serve run on a different platform entirely. A model the engineering team can maintain matters more than any framework name. — Regulation: a model the company trained itself, on data it can trace, is the simplest answer to the AI Act, DORA and data-residency questions.
