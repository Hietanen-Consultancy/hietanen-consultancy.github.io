Hietanen Consultancy · Fuuga · 20 · Every hire waits for signed recurring revenue; gates, not dates

This is the plan we run whether or not we raise. The round on the next slide accelerates it; it does not rescue it. Detail — The rule: a hire waits until signed recurring revenue covers the new payroll about 1.2 times, and the "we run it" contracts are invoiced a year in advance. — Now: the founder's consulting at about £650 a day, four days a week, covers the company's running costs of about £40k a year. The fifth day sells and delivers fixed-scope pilots on a customer's own recorded outcomes. — Capacity before hiring: a pilot bigger than one person brings in senior engineers from our network on contract, paid from that pilot, so the founder is never the ceiling on what we can take on. — £200k signed: two customers whose engines we run at £100k a year pay for the first engineer at £110k fully loaded. — ~£500k recurring: about five customers add a data lead and a seller paid mostly on commission. — ~£800k recurring: a compliance lead — the DORA supplier evidence and ISO 27001 a bank asks of us — and engineers two and three. At seven people the founder stops consulting. — Economics at the full team: about £1.03–1.08m a year of cost. In the year it reaches twenty-two customers, half of them run by us, revenue is about £2.0m — consulting about £0.5m, the recurring lines counted from the month each customer signs — and profit about £0.9m. — Capacity: the 60% margin on running an engine assumes one engineer runs three. AI-assisted operation at about six each is what lets a team of seven carry twenty-two customers; without it, add about three engineers and profit falls to around £0.6m. Why it is slower — A regulated buyer takes about six months to decide, and one seller closes about five a year. The funded plan reaches ten customers in months 13–18; this path reaches them in year three or four. Funding that costs no equity — UK R&D tax relief, about 16% of qualifying spend under the merged scheme; annual contracts paid in advance; paid pilots. Common questions — "Why raise at all, then?" Speed, partners at scale, and a seller in year one rather than year three. The business does not depend on it.
