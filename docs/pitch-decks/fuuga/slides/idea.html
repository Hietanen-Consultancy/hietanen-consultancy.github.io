Hietanen Consultancy · Fuuga · 03

One picture carries the thesis: an open model, made the company's own, ahead of a hosted model where the business is. Detail — The rise: an open model starts a little below a hosted frontier model across general topics. Retrained on the company's own work — its decisions, message formats and code conventions — and grounded in all of its documents, searched in-house, it rises above the hosted model across that domain. The domain is drawn wide on purpose: a lender, an insurer or a payments firm knows a broad slice of its field better than any general model does. — The dashed line: a hosted model given the company's documents in its context improves in the domain. That route exists only for documents allowed to leave the network; classified and confidential material and card data stay inside. The in-house model searches all of them, which is why its curve clears the dashed one. — Skills and facts: retraining teaches skills — how decisions are made, how messages and code are written. Facts that change every week, such as fee tables and contracts, are looked up in the company's documents at the moment of the question (Fuuga's knowledge-graph retrieval). The curve is both together. — The cost just outside the domain: fine-tuning gives up a little general ability there. Training adapters (LoRA) rather than every weight, and keeping general examples in the training mix, keep it small. Every release is compared against the base model on held-out tasks before it ships (fuuga eval --compare; examples/retrain-verification.fsx), so the buyer sees both numbers: what improved and what moved. — More than accuracy: even where the curves are level, an in-house model keeps customer data inside the network, costs the same at any volume, runs offline, and cannot be changed or retired by a vendor. — The curves are illustrations, not measurements; each pilot measures them on the customer's own held-out tasks. Common questions — "What gets worse after retraining?" A little general ability just outside the domain, measured against the base model before release and reported alongside the gains. — "Why not give a hosted model our documents?" Where the documents may leave the network, that helps with facts. It does not teach the model the company's way of deciding or writing, and it is not available for classified or regulated data. — "And questions outside our domain?" The customer's choice: the same model, a hosted one, or both.
