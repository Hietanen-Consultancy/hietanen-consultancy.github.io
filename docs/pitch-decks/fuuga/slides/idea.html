An open model, retrained on the company's own work and grounded in all its documents, gets ahead of a hosted model where the business is. The cost, a little general ability just outside the domain, is measured before release. Detail — Retraining teaches skills and language: the company's own terms, decisions, message formats and code conventions become the model's defaults, not the industry average. Facts that change are looked up in the company's documents when the question is asked. — The dashed line is a hosted model given the documents allowed to leave the network; the in-house model searches all of them. — Adapters (LoRA) and general examples in the training mix keep the loss small; fuuga eval --compare reports both numbers. — Even where the curves are level, the data stays inside, the cost is flat and no vendor can retire the model. — The curves are an illustration; each pilot measures them on the customer's own tasks. Answers — What gets worse? A little general ability just outside the domain, reported with the gains. In the public showcase, none of six benchmarks fell. — Why not give a hosted model our documents? It helps with facts, not with how the company decides, and not for classified or regulated data. — Questions outside the domain? The customer's choice: the same model, a hosted one, or both.
