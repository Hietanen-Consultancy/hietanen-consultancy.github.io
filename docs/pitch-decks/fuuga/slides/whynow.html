Why now

Open weights caught up, regulation arrived, the shapes keep multiplying — and the .NET world still has no path.

Chat was the first product, not the last

Typed decision engines for software to consume, rather than prose for a person to read, arrived this year with serious money behind them. More shapes will follow and nobody knows which wins — which is the argument for owning the platform instead of taking the bet.

Open models are good enough

Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, DeepSeek and Kimi publish weights that, adapted to a company's own cases, beat a generic hosted model at that company's tasks. Fuuga imports all of them.

Sovereignty became a requirement

EU AI Act obligations phase in through 2027; DORA governs ICT risk in finance; boards ask where the model runs and what it was trained on. Provenance is built into every Fuuga checkpoint.

Hardware fits under a desk

QLoRA, offloading and NVMe paging mean a useful model is adapted on one workstation GPU. Quantised export runs it on CPU servers the customer already owns.
