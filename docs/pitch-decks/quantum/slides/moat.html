Defensibility

Code is the cheap part now. The judgement, the evidence and the fleet are not.

Backend independence. One interface over gate machines, annealers, neutral atoms and topological simulation, with OpenQASM and QIR in and out. Whoever wins the hardware race, our customers are already on it.

The rate. Quantum algorithms, five backends and a drone integration in F#, by one engineer directing AI agents, against a test suite in the thousands. A fork inherits the code, not the rate.

Domain integrations. Range-safe sortie splitting, regulatory altitude checks, Crazyflie and MAVLink export. The last mile between an optimiser and a flying fleet is where the work is.

A reputation for not faking it. No silent classical fallback, published problem-size limits, benchmarks against classical baselines. In a field with a credibility problem, that is a brand.

What a fork does not inherit

A regulator's acceptance, an authorisation file, a relationship with an airframe maker. None of those regenerate.

Adoption in a nascent market beats licensing: every developer who starts with our builders is a future planning-API customer.

The hosted planner, the fleet data and the pilots are not in the repository. What a partner buys is accountability: supported builds, current evidence, somebody named when a plan is wrong.

The slide is the business argument. This is the reasoning behind the licence. Detail — Source stopped being the advantage. One engineer with AI wrote this library and a funded team could write another. What stays scarce is judgement, the rate of execution, the evidence that accumulates once a planner is flying, and the people who answer for it. Common questions — "Why give the code away?" A small company cannot defend source against a large one, and patents are published anyway. Releasing first puts the work in the public record as prior art, so nobody fences it off later. — "Won't a large vendor copy it?" F# is not a stack a large vendor lifts into its own product. The realistic risk was never a verbatim copy. — "Isn't owning the code an asset?" Code that has to be defended cannot be thrown away, and every proprietary line is maintenance that narrows what comes next — which matters more than usual in a field whose hardware changes every year. Scope — The licence is the Unlicense and the badge is on the README, so it is a decision on the record rather than something to be discovered.
