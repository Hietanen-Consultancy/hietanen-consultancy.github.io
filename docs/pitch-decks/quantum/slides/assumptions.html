Assumptions and risks

The drivers behind the numbers, and what could break them.

Drivers

  • Embedded SDK licence ACV [£40k] per vendor, 90% retention, four to six signed by year two
  • Planning API [£6k] a month per active operator by year two, most of it through a vendor's product
  • Integration or pilot [£150k] fixed scope, twelve weeks; half convert to a licence or to usage
  • Quota-carrying sellers from month [7] at [5] closes a year each — one in year one, [3] by year three to reach twenty-five
  • Fully loaded engineer cost [£110k] a year; no field-operations headcount

Bracketed figures are the working scenario, not evidenced prices. The first vendor conversation closes them.

Risks and how we answer them

  • The fleets are still small. Which is why we sell to the vendors building for them. Better routing pays at one pilot per aircraft; the ratio is upside, not the thesis.
  • Quantum advantage arrives later than hoped. The hybrid planner earns revenue on classical hardware today; quantum is the upgrade, not the product.
  • Classical optimisers stay better for years. Customers benefit either way through one API, and we publish the comparison ourselves.
  • Liability for a flight. We are a planning tool, not a flight-control system. Every mission is accepted by the customer's pilot-in-command. Professional indemnity, not aviation cover.
