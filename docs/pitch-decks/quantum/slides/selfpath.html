This is the path we run whether or not we raise: nothing is built before a vendor pays. The round buys the hosted planner, the benchmarks and a sales team up front; this path buys them one paying vendor at a time. Detail — Now: the library stays public domain, the four-drone demo is the offer, and the founder's consulting covers the running costs. — First vendor: a fixed-scope integration at about £150k over twelve weeks, delivered with engineers from the network, .NET engineers from Ukraine among them; it pays for itself and for its benchmark. — Two licences at about £40k a year each, with the next project signed, pay for the first engineer. — The hosted Swarm Planning API is built when two vendors agree to route their planning through it. — Without the round: benchmarks on cloud credits or inside paid projects, demonstrations on Crazyflie and ArduPilot's simulator, no hires ahead of revenue. Answers — Why raise at all? To publish the benchmarks and build the hosted planner while the market is forming. The company does not depend on it.
